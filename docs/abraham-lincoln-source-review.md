The public Appendix B pool of 440.3-ft NGVD29 and the LSLI2 430.00-ft zero plus 10.20-ft flat-pool stage differ by 0.10 ft, even before a NAVD88 transformation. The selected 2013 e-chart normal pool is **439.8-ft NAVD88**. At LSLI2's published flat-pool stage, the owner-reported NGS zero would imply **429.88 + 10.20 = 440.08 ft NAVD88**, a **0.28-ft (3.36-inch)** difference from the e-chart's normal pool. Flat pool and normal pool may reflect different references, locations or surveys; their numerical mismatch does not measure actual simultaneous water-surface difference between bridge and gauge. It remains a source-reconciliation question rather than a measured bridge-water offset.

The selected 505.8 NAVD88 low steel and Appendix B's 506.6 NGVD29 have a numerical difference of **−0.8 ft**. The owner's reported NGS shift of **−0.12 ft** applies to the *gauge location*, not automatically the bridge. If −0.12 were provisionally applied to Appendix B low steel, it would give **506.48 ft NAVD88**, **0.68 ft higher** than the selected chart value. This conditional arithmetic demonstrates that datum labels alone do not reconcile the sources; do not treat either difference as a measured bridge datum shift. Check the source page, span and survey basis. The hydraulic-model chord is yet another distinct figure.

An older NGVD29 gauge zero can still define present-day **stage**. Stage is measured relative to the local zero, and changing the geodetic elevation assigned to that zero does not itself change the stage observation. To compare a water elevation to a NAVD88 bridge elevation, a correctly scoped gauge-zero NAVD88 tie must be added to stage. [NGS's sign convention](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/VERTCON3/sign-convention.shtml) is `NAVD88 = NGVD29 + model shift`. NGS's [NCAT/VERTCON tool](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/VERTCON3/) models a location-specific shift; a result should include input coordinates, transformation version and local error. The zero's station epoch/physical benchmark tie also matters. An NGS model output is not by itself a survey of the bridge-to-gauge water-level difference.

### NCAT height export and conservative candidate rule

The owner observed that selecting height conversion on NCAT's multipoint interface changes the CSV to **four columns**, including an orthometric or ellipsoidal height. This corrects our earlier three-column-only description: the [NCAT page](https://geodesy.noaa.gov/NCAT/) publishes a three-column ID/latitude/longitude example in its default text view, while its selectable `Horizontal+height` workflow supports an orthometric input and NGVD29-to-NAVD88 output. Confirm the active height-upload template and units from the selected NCAT interface before exporting data. Gauge-zero elevations in NGVD29 require **orthometric-to-orthometric** conversion; do not choose ellipsoidal height. NCAT does not interchange orthometric and ellipsoidal heights. Use a gauge location for its zero and a bridge location only for a separately sourced NGVD29 bridge height. Keep original feet, converted height/shift, input coordinates and horizontal reference frame, units, gauge-zero epoch, NCAT/VERTCON version, local error and output artifact together in the audit record.

The owner prefers the **smaller supported clearance** when compatible candidate measurements remain in disagreement. For this bridge the 505.8-ft NAVD88 chart low steel is already selected. Once a second low-steel or gauge-zero candidate has been independently transformed to NAVD88 and identified as the **same physical opening/zero epoch**, compare complete candidate clearances and retain the smaller one with both sources in the receipt. Selecting the lowest plausible result cannot make an unsupported cross-datum subtraction valid. For illustration only, 430.00 and 429.88 as *NAVD88* zero candidates with identical stage would differ by 0.12 ft in clearance; the actual published 430.00-ft zero is **NGVD29**, so it is not an eligible NAVD88 alternative as-is. The fourth row remains stage-only while the conversion and bridge-water tie are reviewed.

The owner's proposed Illinois-wide 20-cm maximum for NGVD29/NAVD88 differences is not an approved bound. NOAA [explains](https://vdatum.noaa.gov/docs/est_uncertainties.html) that VERTCON's approximately 2-cm figure is a one-standard-deviation **model accuracy** description, and that rare local NGVD29 network distortions of 20 cm or more exist; neither statement establishes an Illinois-wide bound on the **datum shift** or on total bridge-clearance error. Twenty centimeters is about 0.656 ft (7.87 inches), already greater than the proposed six-inch end-to-end error target if it were an error allowance. Use location-specific converter output and local error instead of one statewide number.

On September 28 the owner approved a **DIRECT, zero-offset bridge-water model**: use La Salle's observed water elevation as the bridge water elevation in an assumption-labeled pilot. The owner expects the difference to be less than one inch; this is an owner estimate, not a field-validated bound. The stations are about **1.0 river mile** apart (225.7 versus 224.7), and physical equivalence over changing flow is not established by distance alone. The previously noted **0.28-ft** flat-pool/normal-pool mismatch compares different reference conventions; it is not a simultaneous measurement of the bridge/gauge water difference. The bridge-water model is now recorded, but the **0.10-ft NAVD88 zero disagreement** must be reviewed before a numerical estimate is activated. The higher owner-reported zero would produce less clearance if it proves applicable; it cannot be treated as an authenticated NCAT output without its settings and source artifact.

## Implemented feed and display

`/api/lincoln` archives the raw NOAA [gauge metadata](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2), [observed stage](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2/stageflow/observed), and [forecast](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2/stageflow/forecast) with hashes, timestamps and a replayable receipt. It checks station/product identity, gauge zero, units, observation order and revisions, issue/generation time, range, source integrity, and forecast run/coverage. Stage is in feet **above the NGVD29 gauge zero**. The strict late threshold is age greater than 24 hours. A failed refresh may show the last accepted stage as historical only.

Forecast direction is calculated from a complete, labeled 24-hour station forecast window, with a 0.1-ft deadband. It describes **LSLI2 only**; it is not a bridge-specific forecast. A missing or stale forecast does not alter stage status. The directory lists the selected **66.0-ft chart clearance** at 439.8-ft NAVD88 normal pool, while calculated clearance remains `DATUM_CONVERSION_REVIEW` with null value regardless of valid stage and forecast. This is not a zero-clearance indication. The older 66.3-ft Corps research value remains in the audit record. The 429.88-ft owner-reported zero and approximately 429.78-ft NCAT API zero are inactive candidates.

The NOAA feed does not supply a per-reading approval/quality flag in this response. An accepted API reading is labeled as an unverified point-quality observation; external quality validation remains open. Bounds 0–45 ft suppress nonsensical stage values in this pilot, not a claim about the range of physically possible floods. `var/lincoln/` stores the local immutable snapshots; `LINCOLN_DATA_DIR` overrides that path.
