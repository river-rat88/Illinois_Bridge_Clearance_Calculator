The public Appendix B pool of 440.3-ft NGVD29 and the LSLI2 430.00-ft zero plus 10.20-ft flat-pool stage differ by 0.10 ft even before transformation. The selected e-chart normal pool is **439.8 ft NAVD88**. At the modeled gauge zero from NCAT, the station flat-pool stage implies about **429.780 + 10.20 = 439.980 ft NAVD88**, about **0.18 ft above** the e-chart normal pool. Flat and normal pools may represent different references, locations or surveys. This comparison is not a simultaneous measurement of bridge/gauge water difference and does not adjust the estimate.

The selected 505.8 NAVD88 low steel and Appendix B's 506.6 NGVD29 have a numerical difference of **−0.8 ft**. NCAT's modeled shift of about **−0.220 ft** is at the *gauge location*, not automatically the bridge. Applying that shift to Appendix B low steel would be unsupported. Check the source page, span and survey basis before comparing these bridge figures. The hydraulic-model chord is another distinct figure.

An older NGVD29 gauge zero can still define present-day **stage**. Stage is measured relative to the local zero, and changing the geodetic elevation assigned to that zero does not itself change the stage observation. To compare a water elevation to a NAVD88 bridge elevation, a correctly scoped gauge-zero NAVD88 tie must be added to stage. [NGS's sign convention](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/VERTCON3/sign-convention.shtml) is `NAVD88 = NGVD29 + model shift`. NGS's [NCAT/VERTCON tool](https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/VERTCON3/) models a location-specific shift; a result should include input coordinates, transformation version and local error. The zero's station epoch/physical benchmark tie also matters. An NGS model output is not by itself a survey of the bridge-to-gauge water-level difference.

### NCAT height export and conservative candidate rule

The owner observed that selecting height conversion on NCAT's multipoint interface changes the CSV to **four columns**, including an orthometric or ellipsoidal height. This corrects our earlier three-column-only description: the [NCAT page](https://geodesy.noaa.gov/NCAT/) publishes a three-column ID/latitude/longitude example in its default text view, while its selectable `Horizontal+height` workflow supports an orthometric input and NGVD29-to-NAVD88 output. Confirm the active height-upload template and units from the selected NCAT interface before exporting data. Gauge-zero elevations in NGVD29 require **orthometric-to-orthometric** conversion; do not choose ellipsoidal height. NCAT does not interchange orthometric and ellipsoidal heights. Use a gauge location for its zero and a bridge location only for a separately sourced NGVD29 bridge height. Keep original feet, converted height/shift, input coordinates and horizontal reference frame, units, gauge-zero epoch, NCAT/VERTCON version, local error and output artifact together in the audit record.

The owner prefers the **smaller supported clearance** when compatible candidate measurements remain in disagreement. For this bridge the 505.8-ft NAVD88 chart low steel is already selected. Once a second low-steel or gauge-zero candidate has been independently transformed to NAVD88 and identified as the **same physical opening/zero epoch**, compare complete candidate clearances and retain the smaller one with both sources in the receipt. The published 430.00-ft zero is **NGVD29**, so it is not an eligible NAVD88 alternative as-is. The owner's screenshot uses different coordinates and is a cross-check, not a second candidate for the LSLI2 zero. The fourth row now displays an assumption-labeled estimate based on the archived gauge-coordinate NCAT response.

The owner's proposed Illinois-wide 20-cm maximum for NGVD29/NAVD88 differences is not an approved bound. NOAA [explains](https://vdatum.noaa.gov/docs/est_uncertainties.html) that VERTCON model uncertainty and the magnitude of the datum shift are distinct; neither provides a statewide limit on total bridge-clearance error. The NCAT response here reports **0.053 m** (about **0.174 ft**) local transformation error. The source gauge-zero epoch, bridge-to-gauge transfer and total six-inch target remain unverified.

## Implemented feed and display

`/api/lincoln` archives the raw NOAA [gauge metadata](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2), [observed stage](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2/stageflow/observed), and [forecast](https://api.water.noaa.gov/nwps/v1/gauges/lsli2/stageflow/forecast) with hashes, timestamps and a replayable receipt. It checks station/product identity, gauge zero, units, observation order and revisions, issue/generation time, range, source integrity, and forecast run/coverage. Stage is in feet **above the NGVD29 gauge zero**. The strict late threshold is age greater than 24 hours. A failed refresh may show the last accepted stage as historical only.

Forecast direction is calculated from a complete, labeled 24-hour station forecast window, with a 0.1-ft deadband. It describes **LSLI2 only**; no bridge-specific forecast is calculated. A missing or stale forecast does not alter the observed clearance estimate. The directory shows the selected **66.0-ft chart clearance** at 439.8-ft NAVD88 normal pool and an assumption-labeled calculated estimate when La Salle stage is available. The estimate is `505.8 − (130.997/0.3048 + stage)` ft, calculated exactly and displayed down to a tenth. The older 66.3-ft Corps research value remains separate. The receipt retains the raw NCAT response hash, source meter values, exact rational foot conversion, stage observation, water elevation, unrounded clearance and model assumption.

The NOAA feed does not supply a per-reading approval/quality flag in this response. An accepted API reading is labeled as an unverified point-quality observation; external quality validation remains open. Bounds 0–45 ft suppress nonsensical stage values in this pilot, not a claim about the range of physically possible floods. `var/lincoln/` stores the local immutable snapshots; `LINCOLN_DATA_DIR` overrides that path.
